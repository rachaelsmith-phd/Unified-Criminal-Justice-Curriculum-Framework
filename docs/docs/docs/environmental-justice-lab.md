# Environmental Justice Laboratory

## Colorado River Justice Laboratory

### Exploring the Intersection of Justice, Environment, Technology, and Community Resilience

The Colorado River Justice Laboratory is an interdisciplinary learning and curriculum-development initiative that examines how environmental systems, resource governance, technological innovation, and community well-being intersect with justice outcomes.

Emerging from doctoral residency experiences along the Colorado River, the laboratory serves as a conceptual space for investigating environmental harms, resource conflicts, sustainability challenges, and justice-centered responses to ecological change.

The laboratory reflects a broader conviction that criminal justice education must extend beyond traditional institutions to include the environmental and technological systems that increasingly influence public safety, community resilience, and human well-being.

---

## Guiding Question

> What does justice require when environmental systems become vulnerable?

This question guides inquiry into how individuals, organizations, governments, and communities respond to environmental challenges including water scarcity, environmental victimization, infrastructure vulnerability, and long-term sustainability.

---

# Theoretical Foundations

The Environmental Justice Laboratory draws from:

- Green Criminology
- Environmental Justice
- Community Resilience Theory
- Human-Centered Design
- Place-Based Education
- Sustainability Studies
- Restorative Justice
- Systems Thinking
- Environmental Ethics
- Community-Engaged Learning

Together, these perspectives encourage students to examine environmental challenges not solely as scientific or technical problems, but also as questions of equity, responsibility, governance, and justice.

## Vision

The Environmental Justice Laboratory seeks to expand how criminal justice education understands safety, harm, responsibility, and justice by recognizing the growing influence of environmental systems on community well-being.

The long-term goal is to prepare future professionals capable of addressing the interconnected challenges of environmental sustainability, technological change, social vulnerability, and justice in the twenty-first century.
