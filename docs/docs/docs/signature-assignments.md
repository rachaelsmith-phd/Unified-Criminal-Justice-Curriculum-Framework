# Signature Assignments

## Authentic Assessment for Justice, Ethics, Technology, and Human Agency

The Unified Criminal Justice Curriculum Framework emphasizes authentic, applied, and professionally relevant learning experiences. Rather than relying exclusively on traditional examinations, students engage in projects, portfolios, case studies, policy analyses, simulations, and innovation challenges that mirror the complex problems facing modern justice professionals.

Each signature assignment is intentionally aligned with one or more curriculum pillars and designed to develop critical thinking, ethical reasoning, technological literacy, communication skills, and professional judgment.

---

# AI Ethics Audit

## Associated Pillars

- Ethics and Professional Responsibility
- Technology, AI, and Society
- Ethical AI Governance

## Assignment Description

Students investigate an artificial intelligence system used within a criminal justice, governmental, educational, healthcare, or corporate setting.

Students evaluate:

- Fairness
- Transparency
- Accountability
- Privacy
- Human oversight
- Potential biases

## Deliverable

Professional audit report and recommendation brief.

## Essential Question

**What responsibilities should never be delegated to artificial intelligence?**

---

# Justice Technology Reflection Portfolio

## Associated Pillars

- Justice Systems Literacy
- Technology, AI, and Society

## Assignment Description

Students maintain a semester-long portfolio examining emerging technologies and their influence on crime, victimization, justice institutions, and society.

Topics may include:

- Facial recognition
- Predictive policing
- Digital evidence
- Platform economies
- Social media
- Generative AI

## Deliverable

Reflective digital portfolio containing analyses, critiques, and personal reflections.

---

# Corporate AI Misconduct Case Study

## Associated Pillars

- Ethics and Professional Responsibility
- Ethical AI Governance

## Assignment Description

Students investigate a case involving corporate misconduct, algorithmic harm, financial technology abuse, data misuse, or AI-enabled deception.

Students evaluate:

- Organizational culture
- Ethical failures
- Governance breakdowns
- Public impacts
- Regulatory responses

## Deliverable

Case analysis and organizational reform proposal.

# Justice Innovation Proposal

## Associated Pillars

- Justice Innovation and Human-Centered Design

## Assignment Description

Potential topics include:

- Reentry programming
- Community safety
- Youth diversion

- Public policy reform

**How can justice systems work better for the communities they serve?**

# Signature Assessment Framework

Across the curriculum, assignments are designed to assess four recurring competencies.

## Ethics

Can students identify and evaluate ethical dilemmas?

## Analysis

Can students apply theory, evidence, and critical thinking?

## Technology Literacy

Can students critically evaluate technological systems and their consequences?

## Innovation

Can students design solutions that promote fairness, accountability, and human dignity?

---

# Portfolio-Based Learning

Collectively, these assignments contribute to a professional portfolio demonstrating:

- Ethical reasoning
- Policy analysis
- Communication skills
- Technology literacy
- Innovation and problem solving
- Professional identity development
- Environmental and community engagement

The portfolio serves as evidence that graduates can move beyond understanding justice systems to actively evaluating, governing, and improving them.
