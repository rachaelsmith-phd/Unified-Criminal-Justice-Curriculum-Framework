## Rachael B. Smith

### Criminologist • Instructional Designer • Adult Educator

Rachael B. Smith is a doctoral candidate in Curriculum and Instruction at Northern Arizona University whose work bridges criminal justice, adult learning, curriculum design, instructional technology, environmental justice, and professional identity development.

For more than two decades, her professional experience has spanned criminal justice education, legal studies, public service, instructional design, workforce development, child welfare, records management, and higher education. Across community college, university, and government settings, she has designed curriculum, developed instructional programs, and taught courses in criminology, criminal justice, legal studies, ethics, policy, and professional practice.

Her teaching and curriculum development are guided by a central question:

> How do we prepare future justice professionals to lead ethically and effectively in increasingly complex technological, social, and environmental contexts?

As both a criminologist and instructional designer, Smith's work explores the intersection of justice systems, artificial intelligence, ethics, human-centered design, environmental justice, and community engagement. Her curriculum philosophy emphasizes critical inquiry, ethical decision-making, technological literacy, and the cultivation of professional identities capable of responding to emerging challenges.

A defining influence on this framework emerged through doctoral residency experiences along the Colorado River, where interdisciplinary study deepened her interests in environmental justice, resource governance, water scarcity, community resilience, and place-based learning. These experiences expanded her focus beyond traditional criminal justice concerns to include the environmental and technological systems that increasingly shape justice outcomes.

## Professional Background

### Criminal Justice Education
Designed, developed, and taught undergraduate and graduate courses in criminal justice, criminology, courts, policy, ethics, race and justice, crime analysis, victimology, white-collar crime, terrorism, and criminological theory.

### Legal Studies & Paralegal Education
Taught and developed curriculum in legal research and writing, criminal law and procedure, torts, contracts, family law, legal technology, e-discovery, and advanced legal writing.

### Instructional Design
Designed face-to-face, hybrid, and online learning environments using evidence-based instructional design principles, accessibility standards, Universal Design for Learning (UDL), and emerging educational technologies.

### Public Service
More than twenty years of professional service within Arizona public-sector organizations supporting workforce development, legal services, curriculum design, compliance, training, records management, and justice-related initiatives.

### Child Welfare
Professional experience supporting child welfare and protective services systems, contributing to interests in vulnerability, advocacy, ethics, trauma-informed practice, and systems accountability.

### Curriculum Development
Developer of the Unified Criminal Justice Curriculum Framework and multiple interdisciplinary curriculum initiatives integrating gence, environmental justice, community engagement, and human-centered design.

## Current Project

The Unified Criminal Justice Curriculum Framework emerged from the convergence of these professional experiences, scholarly interests, and doctoral studies. It represents an effort to reimagine criminal justice education for a future in which technological innovation, ethical responsibility, environmental sustainability, and human dignity must be addressed together rather than as separate concerns.

Through this project, Smith seeks to advance curriculum models that prepare future practitioners, educators, policymakers, researchers, and community leaders to navigate complexity while preserving commitments to justice, accountability, and human dignity.
